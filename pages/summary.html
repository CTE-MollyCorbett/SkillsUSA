Lifetime

Corporal Jackie was initially just a baboon, but upon being orphaned as a baby, farmer Albert Marr picked him up, took him home, and named him "Jackie". Marr then began training Jackie to be a member of his family. A year later in 1914, Marr was drafted, and asked his commanders to bring Jackie along as well, to which they agreed and officially enlisted Jackie, who then became the unit's mascot. Jackie swiftly adjusted to soldier life, with his own uniform, washbasin, paybook, and ration set. Jackie drilled and marched with the soldiers, even going over the top of trenches with them, saluted superiors, stood at ease or at attention, and lit soldier's cigarettes. He ate with a fork and knife, and generally entertained the soldiers during trench stalemates. After the war, Jackie became a celebrity in England newspapers, and collected money for sick or injured soldiers with the Red Cross. Once returned home, Jackie was yet again a celebrity, taking part as the center of parades and achieving the rank of Corporal, a higher rank than any other baboon. Jackie also easily readjusted and thrived back home in civilian life, until tragically dying the day after a fire burned down the Marr farm on May 22, 1921. Albert Marr, his owner, lived until he was 84, dying in Pretoria in August 1973.
